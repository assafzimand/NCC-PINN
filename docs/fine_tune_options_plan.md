# Fixing the Final Joint Fine-Tuning Stage of a Tree-Structured Additive MoE PINN (AToE)

## TL;DR
- Your "fine-tuning makes it worse" failure is the well-documented **warm-start / catastrophic-interference pathology**: unfreezing converged experts under one shared global loss with a fresh, high-learning-rate optimizer kicks already-good experts out of their basin and forces destructive co-adaptation. The single highest-value fix is to **stop doing a full unfreeze at one learning rate** and instead use **gradual unfreezing finest-to-coarsest with discriminative (per-level) learning rates**, anchored by an **L2-SP penalty** toward each expert's converged weights, then finish with a **curvature-aware second-order optimizer (L-BFGS / SSBroyden)** at a tiny step.
- The closest published analog to AToE — stacking / multifidelity FBPINNs (Howard/Heinlein/Stinis) — deliberately **never unfreezes**: each level is frozen permanently once trained. Jointly-trained multilevel FBPINNs (Dolean et al. 2024) instead train all levels simultaneously from the start. This tells you the safe regimes are "stay frozen" or "joint from scratch"; the dangerous regime is exactly yours — converge frozen-and-staged, then suddenly switch to joint.
- Recommended combination, in order of expected payoff: (1) low-LR warmup + discriminative LRs + gradual unfreeze; (2) L2-SP/EWC anchoring to converged per-region weights; (3) prefer fine-tuning only gate/window/output-head/LoRA params over a full unfreeze; (4) switch the joint polish to L-BFGS/SSBroyden. Use the diagnostic ladder in Recommendations to identify which single cause dominates before spending effort.

## Key Findings

**1. Why the joint pass degrades accuracy.** Four mechanisms, all documented, combine in your setting:
- *Optimizer-state reset / destructive first step.* A fresh Adam optimizer at the joint stage has zero first/second moment estimates; the first updates are effectively un-normalized and large, producing a destructive "jump" out of the converged basin. Ash & Adams (NeurIPS 2020, "On Warm-Starting Neural Network Training") show empirically that warm-starting from converged weights damages final generalization versus the staged result, and that the damage is tied to an imbalance in gradient magnitudes at the transition (their "shrink-and-perturb" trick exists precisely to rebalance these gradients).
- *Learning-rate mismatch.* A high LR appropriate for fresh experts is far too large for already-converged experts; it ejects them from their minimum. This is the exact failure ULMFiT (Howard & Ruder 2018) targets.
- *Catastrophic interference / forgetting.* Once-frozen experts, when unfrozen, overwrite the per-region accuracy they had achieved (McCloskey & Cohen; formalized for NN continual learning by Kirkpatrick et al. 2017).
- *Destructive co-adaptation & gradient conflict.* Each expert was optimized against a different local objective (its own window region, or a now-stale frozen background). Under one global loss they begin compensating for each other; in the partition-of-unity overlaps ("collars") their gradients directly conflict. Wang, Teng & Perdikaris (2021, SIAM J. Sci. Comput.) document that competing PINN loss terms produce unbalanced/conflicting back-propagated gradients that drive divergence.

**2. The PINN/DD literature mostly avoids the joint unfreeze entirely.** Confirmed from primary sources:
- *Stacking / multifidelity FBPINNs* (Howard, Murphy, Ahmed & Stinis 2023, arXiv:2311.06483; Heinlein, Howard, Beecroft & Stinis 2024, arXiv:2401.07888): verbatim, "once a network is trained at level i, its parameters (θⁱ and αⁱ) are frozen throughout the next stacking steps … unlike regular multifidelity PINNs where the low- and high-fidelity networks are often trained simultaneously, we perform the stacking process sequentially." **No final unfreeze stage exists** in either paper. Each new level is initialized by transfer-learning from the previous level (θ₀ⁱ = θⁱ⁻¹) and trained on the full PDE+BC loss of the whole composition while everything below stays frozen. Importantly, they train each new level "for the entire solution," feeding the frozen previous level's output as a low-fidelity input to a multifidelity (linear+nonlinear) correlation network — explicitly *not* fitting the residual of the frozen sum.
- *Multilevel FBPINNs* (Dolean, Heinlein, Mishra & Moseley, CMAME 429:117116, 2024): all levels are "summed across all levels and trained simultaneously" from the start — joint from the beginning, never frozen-then-unfrozen.
- *Progressive Domain Decomposition* (Luo, Jo & Kim, Mathematics 13(9):1515, 2025): segments the domain by residual-loss dynamics and "progressively saving the trained model" — successful local models are preserved (frozen), not jointly re-optimized.
- *APINN* (Hu, Jagtap, Karniadakis & Kawaguchi, Eng. Appl. AI 126:107183, 2023): the trainable gate and the sub-nets ARE trained jointly — but from a pre-trained gate initialization that mimics the XPINN hard decomposition, i.e., a warm, structured start rather than a sudden unfreeze of separately-converged experts.
- *PINN-Balls* (Bonfanti, Medina, List, Staeves, Santana & Ellero, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2510.21262): a local MoE with soft gating and learnable domain decomposition trained with **global second-order optimization** throughout, again not a staged-then-unfrozen design.

The lesson: the field treats "freeze permanently" and "train jointly from scratch" as the two safe regimes. Your design sits in the known-dangerous middle.

**3. Best-supported mitigations, mapped to AToE.** The 2–3 most common, best-performing, most compatible strategies are (A) ULMFiT-style gradual unfreezing + discriminative/slanted-triangular LR, (B) anchoring regularization (L2-SP / EWC), and (C) a curvature-aware second-stage optimizer (L-BFGS / SSBroyden). A strong fourth, often the cheapest, is (D) not unfreezing experts at all — only blend/gate/window/output-head/LoRA parameters.

## Details

### Method A — Gradual unfreezing + discriminative & slanted-triangular learning rates (ULMFiT; Howard & Ruder 2018)
- **Mechanism.** ULMFiT introduced three coupled tricks to "retain previous knowledge and avoid catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning": (i) *discriminative fine-tuning* — a different LR per layer, decayed multiplicatively down the stack; (ii) *slanted triangular learning rates (STLR)* — LR ramps up quickly then decays linearly; (iii) *gradual unfreezing* — unfreeze one layer at a time, starting from the last (most task-specific) and adding one per epoch. In the paper's own ablations and in independent reproductions (e.g., FinBERT), gradual unfreezing was the single most important of the three for avoiding the validation-loss blow-up that full fine-tuning produces.
- **Why it fixes your failure.** A low warmup LR absorbs the optimizer-reset jump; per-level LRs let coarse/older experts move very little while fine experts adapt; unfreezing finest→coarsest means the global loss is reconciled by the experts whose job is correction, before the structural backbone is ever perturbed.
- **Math/schedule.** Discriminative: θ_l updated with η_l = η/(decay)^(depth from output). STLR: linear ramp for cut_frac·T iterations to η_max, then linear decay. For AToE: treat each *level* as a "layer group." Map **finest level = last layer (highest LR), root u₀ = first layer (lowest LR)**. Unfreeze finest level first; add the next-coarser level after a fixed number of iterations; unfreeze the root u₀ last (or never). Geometric LR ladder, e.g. η_root = η/8, η_mid = η/4..η/2, η_finest = η, with η itself an order of magnitude below the per-level staged LR.
- **Tradeoffs.** More hyperparameters and a longer schedule; gradual unfreezing is sequential so it costs more wall-clock than a single joint pass. But it directly matches your coarse-to-fine tree.

### Method B — Anchoring regularization: L2-SP and EWC
- **L2-SP (Xuhong Li, Grandvalet & Davoine 2018, "Explicit Inductive Bias for Transfer Learning"; Pattern Recognition 98:107049, 2020).** Penalty Ω(W) = (β/2)‖Wˢ − Wᵀ‖₂² + (β/2)‖W_T̄‖₂², where Wˢ are the converged ("starting-point") shared weights, Wᵀ the current weights, and W_T̄ any task-specific head; this replaces ordinary weight decay (which pulls toward 0) with a pull toward the pre-fine-tune solution. The authors recommend "a simple L2 penalty with the pre-trained model being a reference as the baseline of penalty for transfer learning," and show it "keeps the original control of overfitting, by constraining the effective search space around the initial solution, while encouraging committing to the acquired knowledge."
- **EWC (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017).** L(θ) = L_global(θ) + (λ/2) Σ_i F_i (θ_i − θ*_i)², where F_i is the diagonal Fisher information at the converged weights θ*. EWC is L2-SP with per-parameter importance weights F_i — it lets unimportant parameters move freely while strongly pinning the parameters each expert most relies on. Isotropic L2-SP (F=I) is the special case; the "L2-SP-Fisher" variant in Li et al. is literally EWC's Fisher-weighted distance.
- **Why it fixes your failure.** Both add a quadratic trust region around each expert's converged per-region weights, so the joint pass can still reconcile experts to the new global state but cannot drift far enough to destroy per-region accuracy. This is the most direct cure for the "coarse experts move a lot" symptom.
- **Math/schedule for AToE.** Snapshot θ* for every expert at the end of staged training. Add (λ/2)Σ F_i(θ_i−θ*_i)² to the global PDE loss during the joint stage. Use larger λ (or larger F) for coarser/older experts, smaller for the finest. For the cheap isotropic version set F=I (pure L2-SP). Estimate Fisher from squared PDE-loss gradients on a sample of collocation points (Kirkpatrick et al. estimate Fisher from a few hundred samples). Anneal λ downward late in training if you want a final unconstrained polish.
- **Tradeoffs.** One extra hyperparameter (λ) per group; Fisher estimation adds a one-time cost; too-large λ freezes progress (reduces to "don't fine-tune"), too-small λ gives no protection. Huszár (2017) showed the multi-task EWC penalty can double-count older tasks — for your two-phase (staged→joint) setting this is not a concern. Synaptic Intelligence (Zenke et al. 2017) is an online alternative to the Fisher importance Ω_i if you prefer to accumulate importance during training rather than estimate it post hoc.

### Method C — Curvature-aware second-stage optimizer (Adam→L-BFGS / SSBroyden)
- **Mechanism.** The canonical PINN recipe is Adam for global exploration then a quasi-Newton method (L-BFGS) for the final high-precision polish, because L-BFGS uses approximate second-order (Hessian) information to converge superlinearly in the smooth basin (used widely, e.g., in Separable PINNs, and standard "Adam then L-BFGS" PINN pipelines). Kiyani, Shukla, Urbán, Darbon & Karniadakis (2025, "Optimizing the Optimizer for PINNs and KANs," CMAME; arXiv:2501.16371) systematically compared BFGS, L-BFGS, SSBFGS, and Self-Scaled Broyden (SSBroyden) and report "state-of-the-art results with orders-of-magnitude accuracy improvements without the use of adaptive weights or any other enhancements typically employed in PINNs," tested on Burgers, Allen–Cahn, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, Ginzburg–Landau and Stokes equations.
- **Why it helps the joint reconciliation.** A curvature-aware optimizer with a line search does not have Adam's destructive un-normalized first step and does not need a hand-tuned LR — it is largely insensitive to learning rate. It naturally resolves competing-gradient conflicts (Wang, Bhartari, Li & Perdikaris 2025 show second-order methods achieve implicit gradient alignment between loss terms), which is exactly the overlap/collar conflict in your partition-of-unity model. Running the *whole composition* under one L-BFGS/SSBroyden pass is more stable than Adam for reconciling experts.
- **Schedule.** After staging, do a short low-LR Adam warmup (to rebuild moments / settle the joint state) then switch to L-BFGS or SSBroyden with strong-Wolfe line search to convergence. Watch for premature L-BFGS stalling (documented in multiple PINN studies); an (Adam+L-BFGS) repeat can help.
- **Tradeoffs.** Second-order methods have large memory cost that scales poorly with model size — this is precisely the problem PINN-Balls (Bonfanti et al. 2025) solves via a local-MoE sparse structure to make global second-order training affordable; your additive windowed structure is amenable to the same trick. L-BFGS is sensitive to initialization, so it must follow, not replace, the staged solution.

### Method D — Don't unfreeze the experts: fine-tune only the "glue"
- **Variants.** (i) Freeze all experts; train only the partition-of-unity window/blend parameters w_i(x) and/or a soft gate (APINN-style trainable gate). (ii) Add a thin trainable output head per expert. (iii) **LoRA** (Hu et al. 2021): freeze each expert's weights W₀ and learn a low-rank update W₀ + (α/r)BA with r ≪ dim; B initialized to zero so the joint stage *starts exactly at the staged solution* (ΔW=0) and can only add a small, controlled correction.
- **Why it fixes your failure.** By construction the experts cannot leave their converged basins, so per-region accuracy is preserved; only the inter-expert coupling (the thing that actually needs reconciling) is adjusted. LoRA's zero-init means there is no destructive first step at all.
- **Mapping to AToE.** This is the closest to what stacking FBPINNs already do (they never unfreeze). If your goal of the joint stage is to fix overlap/interface mismatches, the mismatch lives in the blend, not in the expert interiors — so fine-tuning windows/gate/LoRA targets the actual defect.
- **Tradeoffs.** Lower ceiling than a successful full joint refine; if the staged experts are individually mis-converged (not just mis-blended), glue-only tuning cannot fix them.

### MoE-specific guidance (Switch Transformer; sparse upcycling)
- **Sparse upcycling** (Komatsuzaki et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2212.05055) is the canonical "separately/densely trained → joint MoE training" transition: initialize the MoE from a dense checkpoint (experts = copies of the dense MLP) and then train jointly. It works without catastrophic degradation chiefly because the router/gate starts near-uniform and the whole thing is trained as a low-LR continuation of pretraining rather than a fresh high LR — echoing Methods A/C. (Drop-Upcycling, Nakamura et al. 2025, additionally re-initializes part of each copied expert to force specialization and speed convergence — relevant if your experts are too redundant after staging.)
- **Load balancing / expert collapse** (Fedus, Zoph & Shazeer, Switch Transformer, JMLR 23, 2022; arXiv:2101.03961): a router auxiliary load-balance loss L = α·N·Σ f_i P_i prevents "routing collapse" where the gate over-favors a few experts. The authors state: "throughout this work we use an α = 10⁻² which was sufficiently large to ensure load balancing while small enough to not … overwhelm the primary cross-entropy objective. We swept hyper-parameter ranges of α from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁵ in powers of 10 and found 10⁻² balanced load quickly without interfering with training loss." In AToE your windows w_i are a *fixed* partition of unity, so you are largely immune to collapse — but if you make the gate/windows trainable in the joint stage (Method D-i), add a mild balance/entropy penalty (as APINN does with entropy regularization) to stop one expert from swallowing overlaps.

### Other PINN training aids during a joint pass
- *Learning-rate annealing / gradient balancing* (Wang, Teng & Perdikaris 2021) and *residual-based attention (RBA)* (Anagnostopoulos, Toscano, Stergiopulos & Karniadakis 2024, CMAME) reweight loss terms/collocation points; they are complementary and can *help* a joint fine-tune by equalizing gradient magnitudes across regions, but they don't by themselves stop experts from drifting — pair them with anchoring.
- *Causal training* (Wang, Sankaran & Perdikaris 2022, arXiv:2203.07404; CMAME 2024) is valuable when the degradation is concentrated in time-dependent error accumulation, but it is orthogonal to the freeze/unfreeze issue.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Diagnose the dominant cause first (one short run each):**
1. Log per-expert weight movement ‖θ−θ*‖ and the global error over the *first ~50–200* joint steps. If error spikes in the first handful of steps → **optimizer reset / LR** is dominant → fix with low-LR warmup or L-BFGS (Methods A/C).
2. If coarse/root experts move much more than fine experts and error rises gradually → **need anchoring** → L2-SP/EWC (Method B).
3. If error concentrates in window overlaps/collars while interiors stay good → **gradient conflict in overlaps** → fine-tune only the blend/gate (Method D) and/or use a second-order optimizer (Method C).

**Stage 1 — The single most likely fix (do this first):** Replace the full-unfreeze-at-one-LR joint pass with **discriminative + gradual unfreezing**: warm up at an LR ~10× below your staged LR, unfreeze finest level first, add coarser levels one at a time, keep the root u₀ at the lowest LR (or frozen), and use a slanted-triangular schedule. This alone resolves the majority of warm-start degradations and maps cleanly onto your coarse-to-fine tree.

**Stage 2 — Add anchoring:** Layer an **L2-SP penalty** (start isotropic, β per level, larger for coarser experts) on top of Stage 1. Upgrade to **EWC** (diagonal Fisher from PDE-loss gradients) only if isotropic L2-SP over-constrains some experts while under-protecting others. Anneal the penalty down for a final short unconstrained polish.

**Stage 3 — If you want maximum final accuracy:** After the constrained joint pass settles, switch the whole composition to **L-BFGS or SSBroyden** with a strong-Wolfe line search for the final polish (no LR to tune, curvature-aware, resolves overlap gradient conflicts). Mind memory; exploit your windowed sparsity (PINN-Balls-style) if it blows up.

**Stage 4 — If Stages 1–3 still regress, don't unfreeze at all:** Fall back to **glue-only tuning** — freeze experts, train windows/gate and/or per-expert **LoRA** corrections (zero-init, so no first-step jump). This is provably non-destructive to per-region accuracy and matches what stacking FBPINNs already do successfully.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:**
- If even Stage 1 with a tiny LR degrades within 10 steps → your problem is not LR but co-adaptation; jump to Method D.
- If anchoring with any λ that protects accuracy also prevents *any* global improvement → the staged solution is already near the joint optimum; **skip joint fine-tuning entirely** (the stacking-FBPINN verdict).
- If the global error only improves with large coarse-expert movement → your staged root u₀ was under-trained; fix the staging, not the fine-tuning.

## Caveats
- The strongest direct analog (stacking/multifidelity FBPINNs) **never tested a full unfreeze**, so there is no published head-to-head saying "unfreeze + fix X beats staying frozen" for FBPINNs specifically; the recommendation to attempt a *guarded* joint refine is an extrapolation from the transfer-learning/continual-learning literature (ULMFiT, L2-SP, EWC) and general PINN optimizer practice, not from a PINN paper that solved exactly your problem.
- Ash & Adams' warm-start gap was shown on image classifiers; later work argues the gap can vanish under standard practice with proper LR/augmentation — so the magnitude of your warm-start penalty is setting-dependent, reinforcing the need for the Stage-0 diagnostic rather than assuming a cause.
- EWC's quadratic penalty has known theoretical limitations for >2 sequential tasks (Huszár 2017); for your single staged→joint transition this is not an issue, but don't extend the same Fisher across many successive refine rounds without re-deriving it.
- Second-order optimizers (L-BFGS/SSBroyden) deliver the headline "orders-of-magnitude" gains in Kiyani et al. on single-network PINNs; their behavior on a globally-coupled multi-expert composition with frozen/anchored blocks is less characterized — treat the memory cost and possible early stalling as real risks.
- "Discriminative LR per level" and "EWC λ per level" both add hyperparameters; budget for a small sweep, and prefer the cheapest effective option (isotropic L2-SP, glue-only tuning) before the expensive ones.
